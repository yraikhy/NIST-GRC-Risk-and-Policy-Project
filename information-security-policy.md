# Information Security Policy — Portfolio Summary

## 1. Purpose

This policy outline translates the case-study risk findings into governance and security recommendations for the fictional manufacturing company. It is structured around the five functions of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover.

## 2. Scope

The proposed policy applies to employees, management, contractors, temporary staff, suppliers, and third parties who access company information, systems, or facilities.

Critical systems and information include the Manufacturing Control System (MCS), customer ordering website, business databases, employee and financial records, proprietary product designs, production schedules, and inventory records.

## 3. Identify

- **Governance and accountability:** Senior management should provide oversight of information security and risk management. The IT administrator should coordinate implementation of controls and report material risks to management.
- **Asset management:** Maintain an inventory of hardware, software, applications, network components, information assets, and third-party managed systems.
- **Business-critical services:** Identify and prioritise the MCS, customer ordering services, and key databases based on their operational and business importance.
- **Risk assessment:** Conduct periodic assessments and reassess risks following significant technology, supplier, or business changes.
- **Risk treatment:** Document risk decisions, owners, treatments, and management approvals. Treatment options may include mitigation, transfer, acceptance, or avoidance.
- **Supplier risk:** Assess vendor-provided systems and services before onboarding and periodically thereafter. Set expectations for secure access and incident notification.

## 4. Protect

- **Identity and access management:** Assign unique user accounts, enforce least privilege, and review access when roles change.
- **Authentication:** Use multi-factor authentication, particularly for remote access and privileged accounts.
- **Remote access:** Restrict sensitive system access to approved, managed devices and secure access methods.
- **Awareness and training:** Provide role-based training on phishing, social engineering, handling sensitive information, incident reporting, and new systems.
- **Data protection:** Encrypt sensitive information and restrict direct access to databases and proprietary design files.
- **Secure configuration and maintenance:** Establish configuration baselines, controlled change processes, software updates, and security patching.
- **Network and application security:** Use appropriate firewalls and endpoint protection; apply secure coding practices, input validation, and network segmentation.
- **Vendor maintenance:** Approve, secure, monitor, and log remote maintenance sessions. Retain maintenance records.

## 5. Detect

- Monitor network traffic, system logs, user activity, and application behaviour for suspicious events.
- Perform vulnerability scanning and review findings for remediation.
- Establish documented detection and reporting procedures so employees know how to raise concerns.
- Analyse anomalies to determine their potential effect on confidentiality, integrity, availability, operations, and safety.

## 6. Respond

- Maintain an incident-response playbook defining roles, responsibilities, escalation paths, and communication procedures.
- Notify senior management of significant incidents and communicate with affected customers, suppliers, and other stakeholders when appropriate.
- Investigate incidents to establish root cause, scope, affected systems, potential data exposure, and operational impact.
- Contain incidents by isolating affected systems, then remediate the underlying cause.
- Record lessons learned and use them to improve controls, procedures, and policy.

## 7. Recover

- Maintain recovery plans for critical systems and business operations.
- Prioritise restoration of essential services, including manufacturing and customer ordering.
- Maintain backups and test restoration procedures regularly.
- Communicate appropriately with customers and stakeholders during recovery.
- Update risk assessments, policies, and recovery plans based on lessons learned.

## 8. Review and assurance

Management should review this policy periodically and following significant incidents, changes to critical systems, or material supplier changes. Control effectiveness should be assessed through evidence such as access reviews, vulnerability reports, training records, supplier reviews, maintenance logs, incident records, and backup restoration tests.

> This is a proposed policy outline for an academic case study, not a legally approved policy or evidence of implemented controls.
